# Georgian

The Georgian corpus - official translation - is here: 25 masters translated from
the Russian masters in `ru/`, plus the Manifesto in `_manifest/ka-manifest.md`.

Translated from Russian, not from English, and not from the earlier round of
Georgian pages that still sits on the site. Those pages predate the rewriting of
the Russian masters - the Declaration was rebuilt into five parts and eleven
articles - and they are not a reference text.

Georgian is not one of the authentic languages of the UN Charter or of the two
Covenants; Article 53 of the Covenant on Civil and Political Rights names the
five, and Georgian is not among them. So legal terms here are not quoted from an
original. They are taken from the second source that Article 13 of our own
Declaration prescribes for such languages: the official Georgian texts published
in the Legislative Herald of Georgia (`matsne.gov.ge`) when Georgia ratified the
Covenants and the European Convention, plus the Constitution of Georgia and the
Civil Code of Georgia, both of which are originals rather than translations.
Every quotation used was checked against the raw text of the source, not against
a summary of it.

Every terminology decision, with its source, is recorded in
`_СЛОВАРЬ_РЕШЕНИЙ.md` in this directory. The decisions that had no canonical
source and had to be built are collected there separately and marked `[СЛАБОЕ]`:
those are the ones a native reader should look at first.

Three Georgian-specific points are set out in that file and matter more than they
look. Our Charter is `ქარტია` and never `წესდება`, because Article 29 of the
Civil Code makes `წესდება` the compulsory constitutive document of a Georgian
non-commercial legal person - exactly what Article 4 of the Declaration denies
that we are. `ხალხი` is the word for a people and `ერი` only for a nation, a
distinction the Constitution itself draws between Article 3 and its preamble.
And the expression of a collective will is `ნების გამოხატვა`, never
`ნების გამოვლენა`: Article 50 of the Civil Code makes the latter a transaction.

One defect of the official Georgian translation of the Covenants is recorded
rather than reproduced: in Article 1(1) it says `პოლიტიკური განვითარება` where
the authentic texts say cultural development. Our quotation follows the
authentic texts.

What still needs a native Georgian reader: the voice texts - the Manifesto, 03,
17, 23, 31. The legal documents are closed by the accuracy of citation and are
verifiable without a native speaker; intonation is not.
