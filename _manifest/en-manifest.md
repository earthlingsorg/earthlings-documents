# MANIFESTO OF BELONGING

A person is born into a world that has already decided who they belong to.

They are given a citizenship, a state, and a social order they are counted as part of. They can leave, change citizenship, obtain a different passport. But most often they remain inside a collective identity formed before they were born.

**But a person can choose for themselves who to belong to.**

We start from a different principle: belonging should be the free choice of every person.

A person has the right to choose more than where to live and what to believe. They have the right to choose those with whom they share a life, rules, responsibility, and a future.

Belonging should not come down to taking part in a civic organization, a political party, or a social network. It can mean a bond with a community of people who have decided to be together, who recognize one another as equals, and who govern their common life between them.

No one should belong to a particular community merely because it came into being before them.

The internet is changing the very way people come together. People build knowledge and software together, maintain shared databases and maps, form professional and research networks, monitor elections, conflicts, and environmental risks, and create digital commons.

Decentralized organizations have appeared in which the participants themselves set the rules, the membership, the resources, and the way decisions are made. All of these forms widen the space of human self-organization.

But they have one thing in common: people come together around something.

Around source code. Around knowledge. Around a profession. Around a shared resource, a particular task, a project, a set of rules, or an economic interest. Even the most advanced digital communities usually exist in order to make or manage something together.

We propose the next step: to come together not around a common cause, but with one another.

Not only to produce knowledge, manage resources, or build projects, but to build a lasting bond between people. In such a bond the common cause is not the reason for belonging. On the contrary, common causes, knowledge, resources, institutions, and culture become its result.

We are not coming together because we already have a project in common.

**Our project is to be together.**

This is how a different kind of community begins: not an organization founded for a purpose set in advance, but a people in which belonging becomes the ground of a shared life.

This is not a rejection of the forms of self-organization that already exist. On the contrary, the Earthlings people will be able to create open production, shared knowledge, professional networks, digital commons, decentralized organizations, and joint economic ventures. But all of it will happen inside a more fundamental bond - belonging to one people.

In this we see the next step in human self-organization: the move from communities in which people come together for something, to a community in which people come together with one another.

When two people recognize themselves as part of one "we", they are exercising their right to personal self-determination. Something more than an agreement arises between them. A single choice creates belonging. The mutual recognition of many such choices creates a collective subject.

And this choice can be verified. Behind every "we" stands a confirmed living person - not an account and not a bought vote. The right to such belonging existed before; what put it out of reach was the impossibility of confirming a person without revealing who they are. One person, one vote, and that weight cannot be bought, accumulated, or handed to anyone else.

When thousands and millions of people make this choice and recognize one another's belonging, a people comes into being.

A people does not require a common territory, a single language, a common origin, or a religion. It exists because those who make it up exist. It exists not only because they call themselves part of it, but because they are able to act as a "we". Its boundary runs not along the ground but along freely chosen belonging.

A person enters this people by their own will - and by their own will may leave. Belonging cannot be imposed, inherited, or held against a person's will.

Belonging to our people asks no one to give up any other belonging. A person may keep their languages, their cultures, their faith, their citizenship, their family and local ties. We do not replace human diversity with a single identity; we create one more freely chosen "we".

We add to the world something it did not have: a voice that crosses borders without abolishing any of them.

But belonging is not only the right to say:

**"I am with you."**

It is also the readiness to say:

**"You are with me."**

It creates reciprocity. If I belong to you, I recognize your belonging to me. I take responsibility for what happens to us. I am ready to help you not out of compulsion, but because our "we" matters to me.

Governing ourselves together does not override the dignity and freedom of each of us. Neither a majority nor the institutions of the people can turn belonging into submission. Our "we" exists to widen human freedom, not to trade one imposed dependence for another.

From belonging comes trust.

From trust, relationships.

From relationships, institutions and shared practice.

From these, a people.

Such a people has the right to determine its own economic, social, and cultural development, without surrendering the questions of its internal life to anyone outside it.

A people does not always begin with a state or a nation. It begins with people who understand themselves as a collective subject and recognize their belonging to one another.

We call ourselves Earthlings.

Not because everyone on Earth already belongs to this people, but because we have decided to create it.

We are creating a belonging that cannot be received at birth and cannot be taken away against a person's will.

We are creating a people that a person joins by saying: "I choose to be one of us."

And when enough people make this choice, something will exist between them that did not exist before: not merely a community of people, but a people that has constituted itself.

Look around you: you will see a world of abundance. And yet millions of people live in financial need, with loneliness, and with too few people close to them.

Solving human problems takes more than the right place or the right time. It takes people.

Each of us can become necessary to many - just as many can become necessary to us.

No one will create this people for us.

It begins with the decision to say: **"I choose to be one of us."**

**We choose one another.**
